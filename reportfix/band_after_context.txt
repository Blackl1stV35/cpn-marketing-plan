===== SECTION 4.5 (age bands, baseline paragraph, PDPA paragraph, research table S1-S6) =====
In the FY2025 One Report (a team text search finds no mention of Gen Z), the 2Q26 MD&A and the releases reviewed, CPN publishes no Gen Z share of footfall, age split of The 1 members, definition of a visit, CENTRAL X user count or definition of Gen Z (Central Pattana, 2026a, 2026n; Central Pattana Investor Relations, 2026).
[Proposed] Age bands, defined once for the plan. The plan defines Gen Z and young adults as ages 18 to 29 (a team definition, not CPN's) and measures their visits in two ways. The intercept survey reaches only respondents aged 20 and over (the age of majority), whom the team treats as able to consent alone under the PDPA (younger respondents would need a guardian, s.20), so the plan uses it for ages 20 to 29. The 1 member data cover active members aged 18 to 29, so ages 18 and 19 are seen only there, and only as members (their visits as non-members are not measured). The only published comparator is DataReportal's 14.7% share of the population aged 13 to 24 (team sum of the 13–17 and 18–24 bands; DataReportal, 2025). That band is different from the plan band: it is wider at the young end, because it includes ages 13 to 17, whom the survey does not reach, and narrower at the old end, because it stops at 24 while the plan band runs to 29, so any comparison with it is indicative only. Table 5.2 row C1's 25% is a team assumption for the 18–29 band's share of visits. Research row S2 and Chapter 6 indicator K2 use the 18 to 29 band; research row S4 and indicator K1 use its 20 to 29 part, as a share of visitors aged 20 and over. Age bands above 29 are read beside both as a comparison.
[Proposed] The team builds the baseline between 1 January and 31 March 2027. The1 Central Limited, which its privacy policy names as the company collecting the data (The1 Central Limited, n.d.-a), supplies aggregated outputs by age band (active members, visits and spend per member, CENTRAL X scans), which the team compares with the 14.7% population share of ages 13–24 as an indicative comparator only, for the reasons above. Five pilot centres supply entrance-counter totals and CPN's definition of a visit. An intercept survey of 1,500 respondents aged 20 and over, 300 per centre, is weighted to the footfall profile; its margin of error is about ±2.5 points at a 50% estimate, and the smallest detectable change between waves is about 3.6 points at a 50% base share and about 3.1 points at a 25% base share, borrowed for illustration from the team's assumed 18 to 29 share of visits in Table 5.2 row C1 and not an estimate of K1 (team calculation, simple random sampling, so minimums). The policy names the Data Protection Office, Central Group, as its contact, and whether The1 Central Limited is the data controller is left to the Thai-law reader below.
[Proposed] Tourist research uses The 1 Tourist registrations as a self-selected panel, recording nationality at registration if it is not already recorded (Central Pattana, 2026k), aggregated spend by nationality from payment partners CPN names (WeChat Pay, Alipay and MasterCard; whether they can supply nationality-level spend is an assumption to test) (Central Pattana, 2025g), and exit surveys of 150 respondents per market group at six centres (±8 points per group, diagnostic only; team calculation), benchmarked against TAT figures (Nation Thailand, 2026f, 2026h).
[Proposed] The M5 tracker runs one wave a quarter with 400 respondents per target segment, asking an open unaided question on which developers come to mind for caring about the environment. A vendor specification uses 300 respondents as the minimum and calls quarterly waves the most common cadence (Standard Insights, 2026). At 400 respondents the margin is ±4.9 points at 50% and the smallest detectable wave-to-wave change is 6.9 points (team calculation), so M5 targets are set above that after wave 0.
[Proposed] The team read the Thai Royal Gazette text of the Personal Data Protection Act; no official English translation was found, so a Thai-law reader should confirm these points. Consent must be explicit and separate from other text (s.19), minors who cannot consent alone need a guardian (s.20), data collected from another source need notice within 30 days and consent (s.25), objections to direct marketing must be honoured (s.32), breaches are reported within 72 hours (s.37), and administrative fines reach ฿1 million, ฿3 million or ฿5 million by tier (Personal Data Protection Act, B.E. 2562, 2019). The PDPC fined a computer trading company ฿7 million in 2025 for weak security, a missing breach report and no data protection officer (Tilleke & Gibbins, 2025). CPN's CCTV notice lists security purposes, not people counting (Central Pattana, n.d.-a). The intercept survey reaches only respondents aged 20 and over, in every wave, whom the team treats as able to consent alone (the age of majority under the Civil and Commercial Code; s.20 asks for a guardian for minors).
