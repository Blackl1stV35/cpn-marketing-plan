[Page 6 — Economics Synergies: Needs, Trends, Growth]  by ทัพ
DRAFT SCRIPT
So why is this the right moment for that Gen-Z push? Three forces are lining up. On the needs side, shoppers want genuine one-stop convenience, but increasingly in a “phygital” way — browse online, try the product in person, pay digitally, return it flexibly. Malls have also become social hubs, not just shopping errands.
On the trends side, the experience economy is real and measurable — CPN's own festival and event strategy is credited with part of its recent profit growth. Online retail's share of total sales has been climbing, so omnichannel integration isn't optional anymore. And personalization is scaling fast — Central Group's loyalty platform now runs thousands of targeted campaigns a month, up from just a few hundred before.
And on the macro side, Thai GDP is still growing — just under 2 percent last quarter — the retail market is worth about 4.51 trillion baht, and, this is the key point, new mall supply is limited through 2026. That combination — steady demand, rising experience-led spending, and a ceiling on new competitors — is exactly the kind of environment where CPN's existing scale becomes an advantage rather than a liability.
FULL DETAIL — REFERENCE / BACKUP
•  	Market Needs: one-stop convenience + “phygital” journey — browse online, try in person, pay by mobile, return flexibly (Inside Retail Asia, 2025). Emotional shift: malls as social/lifestyle hubs, eroding the old 70:30 retail-to-F&B space benchmark. Younger shoppers favour digital/social integration; older shoppers value tactile in-person experience; safety, cleanliness and visible sustainability now read as baseline expectations, not differentiators.
•  	Market Trends: CPN's “Festival & Experience Economy” strategy — 2026 Songkran campaign drew 15M+ participants, credited for part of Q1 2026 profit growth (Central Pattana, 2025b; Nation Thailand, 2026a). Online share of Thai retail sales rose from 20.45% to 23.50% between 2022–2024 (Nation Thailand, 2025a) while offline stayed nearly flat. 2026 official tourism-arrivals target: 36.7M, but Chinese arrivals specifically remain well under their pre-pandemic peak (Thai Examiner, 2026a). The 1 platform: thousands of hyper-targeted campaigns/month via Adobe Experience Cloud, up from a few hundred (Accenture, 2021).
•  	Market Growth: Thai GDP +1.9% in Q2 2026 (down from 2.8% in Q1), full-year 2026 forecast midpoint ~2.2% (Nation Thailand, 2026b). Total retail market ฿4.51 trillion (2024); the mall format specifically runs closer to 9% CAGR per industry analysts (PS Global Consulting, 2026). Bangkok retail leasable stock ~8.25M sqm by end-2025, with only limited new supply due in 2026 (CBRE Thailand, 2026) — supply is no longer racing ahead of demand, which favours modernised assets like CPN's over ageing stock.
[Page 7 — SWOT Analysis]  by ทัพ
DRAFT SCRIPT
Putting that into a SWOT. Strengths: brand equity built around “Center of Life,” not just retail; a nationwide network no single rival matches; about 510 million visits a year; and data-driven marketing through The 1 loyalty platform.
Weaknesses: CPN is still heavily dependent on physical retail, which means a high, continuous cost to keep that premium experience current, and there's a real tension between operating at this scale and staying as agile as smaller, digital-native competitors.
Opportunities: sustainability as a genuine differentiator — that Net Zero 2050 commitment again — plus data personalization at scale, tourism diversification beyond China, and further mixed-use expansion. And threats: e-commerce and social commerce continuing to take share, Thai household debt sitting at close to 87 percent of GDP, which pressures discretionary spending, and an uneven tourism recovery.
None of this is unique to CPN — but how it responds to the opportunities column is really what the rest of our strategy section addresses.
FULL DETAIL — REFERENCE / BACKUP
•  	Strengths (all independently cited): “Center of Life” brand equity (Central Pattana, 2024a); nationwide network — 44 centres + 16 community malls (Central Pattana, 2026a); ~510M visits/year (Central Pattana, 2026a); data-driven marketing via The 1 (Central Retail Corporation, 2025); experiential marketing — thousands of yearly events: Songkran, Lunar New Year, Christmas, Pride, countdown (Central Pattana, 2025b); omnichannel integration — Chat & Shop as an O2O bridge (Nation Thailand, 2021); international tourist marketing — privileges/airline partnerships (Central Pattana, 2025a). [Page 8 — Competitive Landscape]  by ลิงค์
DRAFT SCRIPT
So who's actually competing with CPN? By Q2 2026, CPN operated 45 shopping centers and 16 community malls — about 2.4 million square meters of leasable area — plus office towers, hotels, and residential projects. No single competitor matches that breadth. Instead, you get specialists.
Siam Piwat — Siam Paragon, ICONSIAM, Siam Center — owns the ultra-luxury, tourist-facing corner, estimated at around 70 percent of Thailand's luxury retail spend. The Mall Group and Terminal21 sit more upper-middle, entertainment-anchored, and often single-city or single-property focused. Seacon Square anchors the mass-market, single-metro corner in eastern Bangkok.
Plotted against those two axes — price tier and geographic reach — CPN is the only player that shows up in the center, serving every tier at national scale. That's the real competitive insight: everyone else has picked a corner. CPN didn't have to.
FULL DETAIL — REFERENCE / BACKUP
•  	Full competitive scale: 45 shopping centres + 16 community malls, ~2.4M sqm net leasable area, 11 office towers/hotels/residential projects by Q2 2026 (Bangkok Post, 2026a). •  	Siam Piwat: Siam Paragon, Siam Center, Siam Discovery, ICONSIAM; ultra-premium/luxury, tourist- and Gen-Z-focused; ~70% of Thailand's luxury retail spend (a single-source estimate, not independently corroborated) (Inside Retail Asia, 2026).

[Page 9 — Product Offering]  by ลิงค์
DRAFT SCRIPT
So what does CPN actually sell, beyond square meters of retail space? Four physical asset types. Shopping centers are the core — 44 of them, about 2.3 million square meters — and the newest flagship, Central Phaholyothin, opens in Q4 2026 as the anchor of a planned new Bangkok business district. Community malls handle the smaller, neighborhood, daily-convenience format. Central Village is Thailand's first international luxury outlet, a joint venture with Japan's Mitsubishi Estate. CPN earns fee income managing its own REIT — essentially recycling capital from mature buildings into new development. And LEAD, its incubator, has supported over 200 Thai brands. So the “product” isn't just malls — it's an ecosystem that captures value on both the physical and data side.

[Page 10 — Keys to Success and Critical Issues]  by ลิงค์
DRAFT SCRIPT
Pulling this together — what actually determines whether CPN wins from here? On the “pursue” side: strengthen financial health, prioritize prime locations — Central Phaholyothin sits on some of the most expensive land in Thailand — keep building tenant relationships, lean harder into data-driven marketing, and formalize ESG leadership rather than treating it as a side initiative.
On the “avoid” side: mitigate e-commerce erosion, buffer against household debt and softening consumer confidence, diversify beyond Bangkok — because CPN keeps opening major malls there even as coverage flags weak consumption — and manage the capital intensity of these mega-projects carefully, since some of that record profit has come from one-off gains, not pure operating strength.
This slide is really the bridge between Chapter 2 and Chapter 3 — it's the same story as the SWOT, but framed as decisions rather than description.
FULL DETAIL — REFERENCE / BACKUP

Next, we divide CPN's target markets into four main groups.
The primary target is urban middle- to upper-income working adults and families. This group is CPN's main customer base because they regularly visit shopping centers for shopping, dining, services, and family activities.
