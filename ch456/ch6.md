## 6. Controls

This chapter turns the Chapter 4 tactics and the Chapter 5 forecasts into controls for FY2027 (1 January to 31 December 2027): a roadmap with stage gates and release rules (6.1), an organization with named owners (6.2), and a contingency plan with numeric triggers (6.3). Items marked [Proposed] are team proposals, not CPN practice, and the Chapter 5 values behind the control states are team forecasts, not company guidance. In the documents the team reviewed, CPN publishes no age-group or nationality split of its visits, only totals for all visits (over 510 million) and international visits (over 70 million) (Central Pattana, 2026a); this is a negative finding, not a citation. The M1, M2 and M5 targets below are provisional and are confirmed at gate G1, after the Section 4.5 baseline due by 31 March 2027. The team thesis, that CPN is using its capital to capture Gen Z share through sustainable positioning, is tested at gates G1 and G3.

### 6.1 Implementation

The plan measures first and spends second: Table 6.1 sets the quarters and Table 6.2 sets the gates at which money is released. CPN reported FY2025 results on 23 February 2026 and 2Q26 results on 10 August 2026, and its calendar lists a SET-organised 3Q26 earnings call on 16 November 2026 (CPN's own call is not yet listed), so Finance reruns the Chapter 5 model at each release (Central Pattana, 2026g, 2026p; Central Pattana Investor Relations, n.d.-a). CPN has not announced the 3Q26 MD&A filing date, so the model is rerun when the MD&A appears; CPN filed the 2Q26 MD&A on 10 August, four days before its 14 August call, and the 1Q26 MD&A on 15 May, three days before its 18 May call (Central Pattana, 2026g; Central Pattana Investor Relations, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). Openings are CPN targets, not completions. The 2Q26 MD&A lists a project named "The Central" at 1Q27 with net leasable area (NLA) of about 100,000 sq m, which the team reads as The Central Phaholyothin (Central Pattana, 2026g, p. 7), while CPN's October 2025 release targeted completion in Q4 2026 (Central Pattana, 2026b) and the One Report lists December 2026 (Central Pattana Investor Relations, 2026, p. 30). ### 6.2 Marketing Organization

CPN's Management Committee had seven members at 31 December 2025, including a Chief Marketing Officer and a President, Retail and Development, and it approves operating budgets within its authority (Central Pattana Investor Relations, 2026, pp. 264–266). On 8 September 2026 the website showed Dr Nattakit Tangpoonsinthana as CMO since 2023 (Executive Vice President, Marketing from 2006) and Chanavat Uahwatanasakul as CEO, with no head of the retail business named (Central Pattana, 2026r); the One Report's first-appointment date of 1 January 2014 is unreconciled. Executive objectives and key results (OKRs) already include shopper and tenant satisfaction and greenhouse-gas reduction (Central Pattana Investor Relations, 2026, pp. 267–268). CPN's customer relationship management runs on The 1 Central platform (Central Pattana, n.d.-e). The1 Central Limited issues the privacy policy (The1 Central Limited, n.d.-a), and the team treats it as the data controller (inference). CPN states that transactions with Central Group affiliates are at market prices (Central Pattana Investor Relations, 2026, p. 50). In the documents reviewed no description of marketing below the CMO was found (a negative finding, not a citation), so the structure below is proposed.

[Proposed] The CMO sponsors the plan through a Plan Office that owns the dashboard and the gates. Six squads own the objectives (Table 6.4), each led by one function; Finance owns F1. M1 to M5 become key results in the squad leads' OKRs, as CPN's executive OKRs already work. Squads meet weekly, the Plan Office fortnightly, and a Marketing Council (CMO as chair; Finance, Operations, Leasing, Sustainability, Data Protection Office) monthly to move money within a control state. Within five working days of each results release Finance reruns the model and the CEO confirms the state. Each quarter the Plan Office gives the Table 6.6 readings to the CEO-chaired Risk Management Committee, which reports to the Board's Risk Policy Committee quarterly (Central Pattana, n.d.-c; Central Pattana Investor Relations, 2026, pp. 62–63).

#### Worst-case Risks

Table 6.8 stresses the Chapter 5 base case for FY2027E, before the ladder's offsets. The pandemic case replays CPN's own core results: in 2020 CPN closed 34 centres for 45 to 56 days, core revenue fell 27%, core net profit fell 51.6% and administrative expenses fell 23.4%, mainly marketing and promotion; in 2021 core revenue fell a further 8.1% and core profit a further 39.4% (Central Pattana, 2021, pp. 161, 166; Central Pattana, 2022, pp. 169, 176). Occupancy was still 92% at the end of 2020, so the occupancy guard (K13) would not have warned (Central Pattana, 2021, p. 161). The other cases use the 86.3% high contribution margin (Table 5.2 row D6) and 20% tax.

Credit is the constraint to watch in the largest cases (team assessment). TRIS upgraded CPN to AA+/Stable on 26 February 2026. It says the ratings could be revised downward if weaker results or debt-funded investment lead to a sustained debt to EBITDA above 4 times; the ratio was 3.4 in 2025, 4.0 in 2024 and 4.3 in 2023 under a stable AA rating, and TRIS expects it to stay below 4 through 2028 despite ฿62 billion of capex (TRIS Rating, 2026). The 4 times level is a sensitivity, not a limit. On TRIS's 2025 figures, with debt unchanged, an EBITDA fall of about ฿4.6 billion (14%) would take the ratio to 4 times; this headroom is a team calculation used as a yardstick (TRIS Rating, 2026). TRIS put the then AA rating on Alert Negative on 12 July 2021 and restored a stable outlook on 29 December 2021 (TRIS Rating, 2026).
